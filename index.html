Why it works this way
  • Saying it aloud, three times. Repeating the same spoken task is one of the best-supported ways to build fluency. Try clear, then natural, then quick.
  • Ready-made chunks. Fluent speakers lean on memorised phrases. These 200 are exactly that.
  • Low pressure. Speaking in real time and noisy rooms are the barriers autistic learners report most. Here you set the pace.
  • Structure you can see. Explicit, step-by-step practice with sound, sight and speech together helped learners with dyslexia most.
  • Count what you did, not how fluent you feel. Adults with ADHD tend to rate themselves lower than they actually are. The counter shows real reps.
